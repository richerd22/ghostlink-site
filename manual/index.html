1SAFETY AND CARE

GHOST LINK is a small computer in a metal case. Treat it like any booth electronics: steady power, a bit of air, no drinks. Each rule says why.

POWER
Use the official Raspberry Pi 27W USB-C supply (5.1V, 5A), or one just like it. Weaker supplies make the box slow down or restart, and it says Power low. Don't use a damaged supply or cable. Avoid switched power strips someone might turn off; it's harmless, just annoying.
UNPLUGGING
Pulling the power is the normal way to switch it off. The system is read-only and the night is saved every few seconds, so it can't be hurt that way.
HEAT
It has no fan: the case is the cooler. Give it air. Not on top of an amp, not in a closed bag, not in direct sun. STATUS warns when it runs hot (above 75 °C inside).
LIQUIDS
Keep drinks away. It is not waterproof. If it gets wet, unplug it and let it dry fully before power goes back in.
CABLES
Run cables where nobody trips on them and nothing pulls on the ports. Use plain network cables (Cat5e or Cat6). Never plug it straight into one player: then it only sees that one.
THE MEMORY CARD
The system lives on a memory card inside. Never take it out, wipe it or reflash it: that erases the box and every night on it. If something seems broken, contact support first.
OPENING IT
There's nothing inside for you to service except the clock battery (see EXTRAS). Unplug first.
THE DJ'S GEAR
Nothing passes through GHOST LINK: if it dies, the booth carries on. It never sends anything to the mixer. Deck controls stay off unless the owner turns them on.

2WHAT'S IN THE BOX

  • The GHOST LINK box: a Raspberry Pi 5 in a fanless aluminium case.
  • Its power supply: the official Raspberry Pi 27W USB-C supply.

You bring your phone and a network cable that reaches the DJ switch. Anything else is optional: gear to buy.

EXTRAS THAT MAY COME WITH IT

Some boxes come with these. None of them is needed to run.

THE STICKER
The GHOST LINK Wi-Fi name and password, and a QR code to join it. Stick it on the box, on the side you can see in the booth. No sticker? The password came with your box, on the card or email. Change it to your own: STATUS → the lock.
A NETWORK CABLE
A plain Ethernet cable (Cat5e or Cat6), any length that reaches the DJ switch. One end in the box, the other in a spare port on the switch the players and mixer use. Any cable works; a short, flat one packs best.
A CLOCK BATTERY
Keeps the box's clock right while it's unplugged, so the first tracks of a night land on the right date before it finds Wi-Fi. Without one the box still works: it sets its clock from the internet or from the first phone that opens the dashboard.

Two kinds: the official Raspberry Pi RTC battery (rechargeable; the box has to be set to charge it, ask support) or a regular CR2032 coin cell on a plug (not rechargeable; charging stays off, replace it every few years).

CAREFUL. Never put a regular coin cell in a box set to charge its battery.

  1. Unplug the power.
  2. Open the case.
  3. Find the small two-pin socket marked BAT on the board, next to the USB-C power socket.
  4. Push the battery's plug in (it only fits one way). Stick the battery to the inside of the case with its sticky pad, away from the hot chip.
  5. Close the case, plug the power back in. STATUS → BOOTH CHECK → CLOCK BATTERY says if it's working.

3KNOW YOUR BOX

The parts you touch, and what each one is for. The diagram is a schematic, not a scale drawing.

SCHEMATIC, NOT TO SCALE. THE BUTTON AND THE STATUS LIGHT ARE THE RASPBERRY PI'S OWN, SIDE BY SIDE ON ONE EDGE, REACHED THROUGH THE CASE.

ETHERNET
The one network port. Cable it to the switch or DJ router the players and mixer use. This is the DJ network side.
USB-A PORTS
Four: two blue (faster), two black. For optional add-ons, any free port: a USB sound card (SOUND IN and set recordings), a USB MIDI interface (MIDI CLOCK to a lighting desk), or a USB cable to the mixer's USB port (listen-only mixer controls). Nothing is needed here to start.
USB-C POWER
The 27W supply goes here. Push it all the way in.
THE CASE
A FLIRC aluminium case: the whole case is the heatsink, so it runs silent with no fan and gets warm to the touch. It has no buttons of its own: the power button and the status light are the Raspberry Pi's own, side by side on one edge, reached through the case (the light shows through a small light pipe).
POWER BUTTON
The Raspberry Pi's small button. Press once: a clean shutdown in about 10 seconds (the light turns red). Press again: it starts up, dashboard back in about 30 seconds. Unplugging is fine too.
STATUS LIGHT
One light, two colours. Green, solid: running, on a Wi-Fi it knows. Green, slow blink (1 second on, 1 off): running, and it is the GHOST LINK Wi-Fi. Green, fast blink: running, no Wi-Fi yet. Red: shut down but still plugged in (press the button to start). Off: no power. It shows red for a moment when power first goes in, then green.
WI-FI AND BLUETOOTH
Built in, no antenna to fit. Wi-Fi is for phones. Bluetooth only gives off a small signal so the HARD STOP app notices the box; it never connects to anything.
CLOCK BATTERY
Inside, on the board's plug marked BAT, next to the USB-C power. Keeps the time while unplugged. Replace with a CR2032 coin cell when BOOTH CHECK → CLOCK BATTERY says LOW or FLAT. Never turn on battery charging for a coin cell.

BOO: Green means I'm up. Red means I'm asleep but still plugged in. Off means no power, not a sulk.

5SETUP

The first day. Doing it at home first means the booth only gets the easy part. In a hurry? GETTING STARTED is the same thing, shorter.

  1. POWER IT

    Plug in the supply. The green light blinks fast: it's looking for a Wi-Fi it knows, and doesn't know any yet.

  2. WAIT ABOUT 90 SECONDS

    With no Wi-Fi it knows, it becomes its own Wi-Fi, GHOST LINK. The green light slows to a 1-second blink.

  3. JOIN GHOST LINK

    On your phone, join the Wi-Fi called GHOST LINK. The password came with your box (on the sticker or card). A page pops up: tap GOT IT. The dashboard opens at 10.42.0.1. A short tour shows once.

  4. YOUR PASSWORD

    STATUS, type the password that came with the box, UNLOCK, then CHANGE PASSWORD to your own (8 characters or more). It signs every other phone out.

  5. YOUR WI-FI

    STATUS → VENUE → NEW, call it HOME, + VENUE WI-FI with your Wi-Fi's name and password, SAVE. Within a minute the box moves to your Wi-Fi; join it too and open ghostlink.local.

  6. EACH VENUE

    Add each venue the same way (say, THE BLUE ARCH), with its Wi-Fi if you can get it. At each venue the box joins the strongest one it knows. With none around, it is the GHOST LINK Wi-Fi again.

  7. A BACKUP

    STATUS → BACKUP. Keep the file on your phone.

TIP.

Type venue Wi-Fi names and passwords exactly as the venue gives them, capitals and spaces included. Most "it won't join" mysteries are one wrong letter.

AT THE BOOTH

  • Plug into the switch or DJ router the players and mixer use. Any free port. Never straight into one player: then it only sees that one.
  • Router or switch full? Bring a plain unmanaged 5-port switch: move one cable from the router into the switch, cable the switch back to that router port, plug GHOST LINK into the switch.
  • The mixer on the network too gives ON AIR and a better PLAYED. Without it, things still work.
  • Players must be in their normal standalone (LINK) mode. In laptop or controller mode they don't talk on the network.
  • Guest Wi-Fi that keeps devices apart from each other won't work for phones. Use the GHOST LINK Wi-Fi there.

BOO: A spare 5-port switch in the bag has saved more nights than any clever setting.

6EVERY NIGHT

The routine, start to finish. Once it is set up, a night asks for a few minutes before doors and nothing during.

BEFORE YOU LEAVE

  • The box, its power supply, a network cable and a spare.
  • Optional: a small switch, the sound card and its RCA cable, the MIDI interface.
  • New venue? Ask for its Wi-Fi name and password, and plan the night (STATUS → NIGHTS) with STARTS, MUSIC OFF and the lineup, like 22:00 TICK and 23:30 LIL TICK.

AT THE BOOTH

  1. Network cable from the box to the DJ switch.
  2. Power. Wait a minute. Check the green light.
  3. Phone on the same Wi-Fi: ghostlink.local or gl.cold.fyi on a Wi-Fi it knows, or join GHOST LINK.
  4. STATUS → BOOTH CHECK, top to bottom. Confirm the venue if it asks.
  5. DECKS: load a track on each deck. Title, art, waveform.
  6. Only if wanted tonight: ● RECORD for a set the DJ agreed to; SHOW CONTROL for lights or visuals; AUTO-STOP for a hard curfew.
  7. Share: tap GHOST LINK at the top for the QR. Crew who need controls get tonight's crew code, not the password.

DURING

Nothing to do. Alerts show on every phone. If anything looks odd: STATUS → LISTEN ONLY, no password needed. Last resort: pull the power.

BOO: The best nights are the ones where you forget I'm here.

AFTER

  • PLAYED: COPY TRACKLIST, PLAYLIST, RECAP IMAGE. Share a tracklist publicly only if the DJ says yes.
  • Unplug the box. That's the normal off.

9SOUND IN AND RECORDINGS

The DJ network carries data, not sound. A USB sound card gives the box ears: it hears the mixer's actual sound. NOT YET TRIED ON REAL GEAR

HOOK IT UP

  1. A class-compliant USB sound card (for example a Behringer UCA202) into a USB port on the box. It's found within 5 seconds.
  2. An RCA cable from the mixer's REC OUT (or BOOTH OUT) to the card's INPUT. REC OUT already taken: an RCA Y-splitter.
  3. Play music. NETWORK → SOUND IN: the L / R meter moves. Aim for loud parts around -12 dB.
TIP.

Meter flat with music playing? Check the cable is in the card's INPUT, not its OUTPUT, and that REC OUT is turned up.

WHAT YOU GET

  • A live level meter.
  • A SILENCE warning: below -50 dBFS for 8 seconds after music. Quiet for 5 minutes after STOP ALL DECKS. It works on laptop nights too.
  • SET RECORDINGS, only when you choose.

RECORDINGS: ONLY WHEN YOU CHOOSE

CAREFUL.

A set belongs to the DJ who played it. Record only a set the DJ agreed to.

  • ● RECORD (with the DJ's name on it) arms recording for whoever is ON THE DECKS. It needs the password or tonight's crew code.
  • It records while music plays, and ends when ON THE DECKS changes, at the night change, after 10 minutes of silence, or on ■ STOP RECORDING. Anyone at the venue can stop; no password.
  • The next DJ must be chosen again. Nothing ever records without a tap.
  • A red ● REC shows at the top of every phone while it records.
  • Files are lossless FLAC, about 0.5 GB an hour, with a tracklist timed from the file's start. Download and DELETE need the password. The oldest go by themselves when under 3 GB is free; never the one being written.

Not the same as the box's session logs, which hold deck data for support and never any sound.

BOO: Recording is opt-in, every set, every time. When TICK hands over to TICK JR, I stop and wait to be asked again.

10DECK CONTROLS AND AUTO-STOP

The one chapter where the box stops just watching. Read the two boxes below first.

OFF BY DEFAULT.

Out of the box GHOST LINK only listens. DECK CONTROLS are the one place it may send commands to players, and only after the owner turns them on with the owner password (the crew code can't). They're refused from WATCH FROM HOME.

NOT YET TRIED ON REAL GEAR.

The XDJ-XZ ignores every command. CDJ-3000s haven't been tried yet. Try your own decks at home before a night that depends on it. Everything else in GHOST LINK works the same either way.

THE BUTTONS

Every button: tap to arm, then hold 1 second. That's on purpose: nobody stops a set by brushing a phone.

  • STOP ALL DECKS. A network stop sends a deck back to its cue point.
  • Per deck: PLAY / STOP, FROM CUE, SYNC ON / OFF, MASTER.
  • LOAD: pick a USB or SD on the network, search it, LOAD ONTO a deck. Never onto a playing deck.
  • It asks first (YES / CANCEL) before playing over music on air, replacing a loaded track, SYNC on a deck on air, and the like.
  • The deck plays through its own channel: the fader has to be up.
  • Sent isn't obeyed: each command is checked against the decks a few seconds later, and the result goes in EVENTS.

AUTO-STOP AT MUSIC OFF

  1. Set MUSIC OFF: in the night's plan, or as the venue's usual time under MUSIC AT THIS VENUE.
  2. DECK CONTROLS → ARM FOR TONIGHT (hold). It resets the next night.
  3. Every phone gets a bar at 10, 5 and 1 minute with CANCEL AUTO-STOP. Then every deck stops once.

It never fires late (a box that was off at the time doesn't stop the music when it comes back), and never fights a DJ who plays again. HARD STOP CAN STOP IT (off by default) lets the HARD STOP app stop the music in the last 10 minutes; the app can always cancel.

HOUSE SETTINGS

A club-standard deck setup, editable by the owner, sent to stopped decks between DJs with SEND TO STOPPED DECKS. BOOTH CHECK → DECK SETUP shows decks whose jog mode or firmware is off; the other settings aren't reported back by the decks.

Never possible over the network: scratching, seeking, effects, or anything on the mixer.

12UPDATES AND ROLLBACK

You update the box yourself, from a phone: STATUS → THE BOX → UPDATES, with the owner password. And if you don't like what you got, the way back is one button.

WITH INTERNET
CHECK, then INSTALL (tap, then hold 1 second), or WHEN THE MUSIC STOPS. GHOST LINK restarts, about 20 seconds.
NO INTERNET
Get the update file from GHOST LINK support onto your phone. Join the box's Wi-Fi, INSTALL FROM FILE, pick the file, then INSTALL.
SAFE BY DESIGN
Only files signed by GHOST LINK install. Nothing installs while a deck is playing. The new version is tested on the box first, and if it doesn't stay healthy for two minutes the box goes back to the old one by itself.
GOING BACK
GO BACK TO PREVIOUS puts the last version back. It takes under a minute and works with no internet. Your venues, nights and recordings are kept either way.
NEVER
Never wipe or reflash the memory card yourself. It erases the box and every night on it.
TIP.

Keep a BACKUP on your phone (STATUS → BACKUP). It carries venues, nights, played lists and the password to a replacement box.

13PRIVACY

Everything runs on the box. No account, no cloud, no analytics. What it keeps, and who can see it:

PLAYED TRACKS
Every track that went on air, per night, with the DJ's name as set on the box. Anyone on the box's Wi-Fi can see PLAYED. WATCH FROM HOME shows it only when the tracklist is shared, tonight only.
VENUES AND WI-FI
Venue details and saved Wi-Fi passwords stay on the box. Wi-Fi passwords only show to phones unlocked with the owner password.
RECORDINGS
Only a set someone chose to record. Downloading or deleting needs the password.
THE BLACK BOX
A log of what the box saw (devices on the DJ network, Wi-Fi it scanned, dashboards that connected), kept on the box for after a night. It leaves the box only if you download it.
SESSION LOGS
Deck data from each session (never sound), for support. Old ones are pruned.
USB STICKS
A USB's name and date, so a set can be named by itself next time.
ARCADE
The names typed on the high-score board.
THE PASSWORD
Stored as a hash, not the password itself.

WHAT LEAVES THE BOX

  • WATCH FROM HOME, only while it's on: a view-only link through a Cloudflare tunnel. Off by default and after every restart.
  • gl.cold.fyi, only if a GitHub token is set on the box: the box's address on its Wi-Fi, the room name, and a one-way hash of the Wi-Fi's public internet address (never the address itself), so phones on the same Wi-Fi can find it.
  • Bluetooth: a small signal with the box's address on its Wi-Fi, so the HARD STOP app notices it.
  • SHOW CONTROL and OSC OUT: only to the computers you list.
  • HARD STOP: the HARD STOP app on the same Wi-Fi reads now playing and the night's state.

14WHERE THE DATA COMES FROM

From the PRO DJ LINK network your players and mixer already use to talk to each other: what's loaded, playing and on air, tempo, beats, and the track details the players share (title, artist, key, waveform and sections from rekordbox analysis). GHOST LINK reads it with beat-link, an open-source library by Deep Symmetry built by studying that network (Beat Link Trigger works the same way).

What that means: Pioneer DJ / AlphaTheta haven't published this protocol and haven't certified GHOST LINK. A player firmware update can change something before we catch up; KNOWN ISSUES says when it does.

What it doesn't do: it doesn't copy or read your music files, doesn't need rekordbox running, and keeps only what went on air (and a set recording, only when you choose one). Out of the box it only listens; deck controls stay off unless you turn them on.

With a USB sound card, it also hears the mixer's REC OUT: the actual sound, not just the data.
