POWER

The box is a Raspberry Pi 5. It needs a proper 5 V, 5 A supply: the official Raspberry Pi 27W USB-C supply. Phone chargers and USB ports on power strips are often too weak; the box then says Power low on STATUS. Push the plug all the way in, and keep it off a switched power strip someone might turn off.

NETWORK

  • Network cable: any Cat5e or Cat6 cable long enough to reach the DJ switch or router.
  • Small switch: a plain, unmanaged 5-port switch (for example a TP-Link TL-SG105 or Netgear GS305). For a booth where the DJ router has no free port: move one cable from the router to the switch, run a cable from the switch back to that router port, and plug GHOST LINK into the switch.

SOUND CARD

Our pick: Behringer UCA202 (or the UCA222, the same small card in a different case). About $30.

  • RCA inputs: the same plug as the mixer's REC OUT, so one plain cable does it. No adapters.
  • No driver: plug it into the box and it's found within a few seconds.
  • Powered by USB from the box.
  • 16-bit, 48 kHz: CD quality.

Why that's enough: GHOST LINK records at 16-bit, 48 kHz, lossless. A mixer's REC OUT is already a finished, loud mix, so a pricier card makes no better recording here.

When a better card would matter: the Behringer UMC202HD (about $80-100) or MOTU M2 (about $170-200) have cleaner converters, proper gain knobs and front-panel meters. Worth it only if the same card is also your studio interface. Both have jack inputs, so you'd need RCA to 1/4" cables. Any USB sound card that needs no driver works.

Set up: record a set.

CABLES FOR THE SOUND CARD

  • Stereo RCA cable, male to male, about 8 ft: from the mixer's REC OUT (or BOOTH OUT, if it's free) to the card's INPUT. Red is right, white is left.
  • RCA Y-splitter, 1 male to 2 female (one per side, so buy a pair): when the venue's own recorder already uses REC OUT. The splitter goes on REC OUT, their cable in one side, yours in the other.

MIDI INTERFACE

Only for MIDI CLOCK: the DJ's tempo to a lighting desk, drum machine or anything with a round 5-pin MIDI IN.

  • Our pick: Roland UM-ONE mk2 (about $40-50). One in, one out, powered by USB. Set the little switch on its side to TAB (no driver). The plug marked OUT goes to the desk's MIDI IN.
  • iConnectivity mio or M-Audio Uno (about $30-40): also work without a driver. Fine for clock.
  • No-name USB MIDI cables (about $10-15): some drop or garble the clock. Fine to try at home; keep a known one for shows.

A desk with its own USB port usually can't plug into the box directly: use the desk's 5-pin MIDI IN. GHOST LINK never sends clock to a Pioneer DJ or AlphaTheta mixer or deck.

Set up: hook up lights and visuals.

MIXER USB CABLE

Many DJ mixers have a square USB-B port on the back. A USB-A to USB-B cable from the box lets GHOST LINK read the mixer's knobs and faders: FX LEFT ON and the trim and EQ notes in GEAR CARE. It only reads; nothing is sent to the mixer. Some newer mixers use USB-C: check the back panel.

CLOCK BATTERY

A small battery on the box's clock keeps the time while it's unplugged, so the night's times are right with no internet. STATUS → BOOTH CHECK shows CLOCK BATTERY: GOOD is fine; LOW or FLAT means it's time for a new one. Ask us before fitting one yourself: the official Raspberry Pi battery is rechargeable and needs charging switched on, and a regular coin cell must never be charged.
